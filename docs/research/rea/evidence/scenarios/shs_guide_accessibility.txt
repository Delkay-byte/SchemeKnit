    - heading "How to Write a NaCCA-Compliant Lesson Plan in Ghana (2026 Guide)" [level=1]
    - paragraph: By Admin ┬╖ Updated 29 July 2026
    - strong: "Quick answer:"
    - text: A NaCCA-compliant SHS lesson plan in Ghana must include four elements copied verbatim from the Standards-Based Curriculum (Strand, Sub-Strand, Content Standard, Learning Indicator), 2ΓÇô3 measurable learning outcomes, activities that span at least two Depth of Knowledge (DoK) levels, differentiation for MA/HP/NS proficiency levels built into the activities themselves, explicit GESI/SEL/21st Century Skills references, and a closing assessment that tests the same indicator the lesson opened with.
    - paragraph: Writing a lesson plan that satisfies GES supervision and reflects the NaCCA Standards-Based Curriculum is one of the most time-consuming parts of a Ghanaian SHS teacher's week. This guide breaks down exactly what a NaCCA-compliant lesson plan needs, in the order it should appear, so you can write one quickly ΓÇö or check that one you already have covers everything.

    - heading "1. Start from the curriculum, not the topic" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Every NaCCA lesson plan begins with four elements pulled directly from the Standards-Based Curriculum document for your subject and year:"
    - list:
      - listitem:
        - strong: Strand
        - text: ΓÇö the broad curriculum area (e.g. Algebra, Reading, Forces and Energy)
      - listitem:
        - strong: Sub-Strand
        - text: ΓÇö the specific theme within the strand
      - listitem:
        - strong: Content Standard
        - text: ΓÇö the broad expectation for learners by the end of the year
      - listitem:
        - strong: Learning Indicator(s)
        - text: ΓÇö the specific, assessable outcome for this lesson
    - text: Copy these four fields verbatim from the curriculum document. Paraphrasing them is one of the most common reasons lesson notes get flagged during supervision ΓÇö GES and NaCCA expect exact wording, not a summary of it.
    - heading "2. Write clear, measurable learning outcomes" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Turn the Indicator into 2ΓÇô3 outcome statements learners can demonstrate by the end of the lesson ΓÇö using observable verbs (identify, calculate, construct, compare) rather than vague ones (understand, know, appreciate).
    - heading "3. Reference Depth of Knowledge (DoK)" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "NaCCA lessons are expected to move learners beyond recall. Structure your core activities so they touch at least two DoK levels:"
    - list:
      - listitem:
        - strong: DoK 1
        - text: ΓÇö Recall (define, list, state)
      - listitem:
        - strong: DoK 2
        - text: ΓÇö Skill/Concept (classify, compare, solve)
      - listitem:
        - strong: DoK 3
        - text: ΓÇö Strategic Thinking (justify, critique, design)
      - listitem:
        - strong: DoK 4
        - text: ΓÇö Extended Thinking (investigate, connect across topics)
    - heading "4. Differentiate for proficiency levels, not for separate lesson notes" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Rather than writing three lesson notes, write one lesson plan with activities that flex across:"
    - list:
      - listitem:
        - strong: MA
        - text: ΓÇö Minimum Achievement (needs more support)
      - listitem:
        - strong: HP
        - text: ΓÇö Highly Proficient (ready for extension)
      - listitem:
        - strong: NS
        - text: ΓÇö Nurturing/Support learners with specific needs (SEN)
    - paragraph: Build this into the activity description itself ΓÇö a good NaCCA lesson plan reads naturally, without needing a label like "For MA learners:" attached to every line.
    - heading "5. Build in GESI, SEL, and 21st Century Skills" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "These three threads run across the whole curriculum and reviewers look for them explicitly:"
    - list:
      - listitem:
        - strong: GESI
        - text: (Gender-Equity and Social Inclusion) ΓÇö activities and examples that don't assume one gender, ability level, or background
      - listitem:
        - strong: SEL
        - text: (Social-Emotional Learning) ΓÇö peer collaboration, self-regulation, respectful group work built into the activity, not bolted on as a separate paragraph
      - listitem:
        - strong: 21st Century Skills
        - text: ΓÇö critical thinking, communication, collaboration, creativity, made visible in what learners actually do
    - heading "6. Choose an assessment that matches the indicator" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Your closing assessment should test the same indicator you opened with ΓÇö a quick oral Q&A, a short written exercise, or a practical task, scaled to the time available in a single period.
    - heading "7. Keep the format consistent with your school's PLC Handbook" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Where your subject has a school-level exam format documented in your school's PLC Handbook (separate from WASSCE structure), make sure your lesson's assessment style feeds naturally into that format over the term, not just the single lesson.

    - heading "Frequently Asked Questions" [level=2]
    - term: What must a NaCCA lesson plan include?
    - definition: Strand, Sub-Strand, Content Standard and Indicator from the curriculum, clear learning outcomes, differentiated activities across proficiency levels, an assessment strategy, and GESI/SEL/21st Century Skills references.
    - term: What is DoK in a Ghanaian lesson plan?
    - definition: Depth of Knowledge ΓÇö a scale from simple recall to strategic and extended thinking. NaCCA expects lessons to move learners beyond recall alone.
    - term: Do I need a different lesson note for each proficiency level?
    - definition: No ΓÇö one lesson plan with differentiated activities for MA, HP, and NS learners, not three separate documents.
    - term: What is the difference between a lesson plan and a scheme of work in Ghana?
    - definition: A scheme of work is a term-long, week-by-week map of topics and indicators. A lesson plan is the detailed document for one specific lesson within that scheme.
    - term: How long should a NaCCA lesson plan be?
    - definition: Typically 1ΓÇô2 pages for a single period, once the curriculum header, outcomes, differentiated activities, and assessment are included.
    - term: Can I reuse the same lesson plan every term?
    - definition: The curriculum header and structure can carry over, but GES supervisors expect activities and assessment to be refreshed to reflect the current class.
